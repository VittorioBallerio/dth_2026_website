# Disadvantages of Open Source Software

Despite its advantages, open source software also has some important disadvantages. One of the main problems is that projects depend on people who are willing to develop and maintain them. Unlike commercial companies, open source projects often rely on volunteers who may have limited time and resources. If these people stop contributing, the software can become outdated or abandoned.

Another disadvantage concerns security. Since the source code is public, anyone can examine it, including people looking for vulnerabilities. Although transparency allows developers to identify and fix problems, it does not guarantee that someone will actually check the code. The XZ Utils incident in 2024 is a good example. A malicious backdoor was introduced into a widely used open source tool after a contributor gained the trust of its maintainer. This showed how much risk can arise when important software depends on very few people.

Open source software can also be difficult to use. Some projects have limited documentation, complicated interfaces or little technical support. This can be frustrating for students or researchers who need reliable tools but do not have the technical knowledge to solve problems themselves.

There are consequences for public institutions too. A municipality may save money by adopting open source software, but it still needs people to install, update, secure and maintain it. These activities require time, expertise and funding.

Therefore, open source should not automatically be considered better or safer than commercial software. Its success depends on active communities, responsible maintenance and sufficient resources.
